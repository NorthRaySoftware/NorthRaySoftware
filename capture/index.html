About

NorthRay Capture is a livestreaming and recording app for Windows, being built in stages towards a real alternative to OBS Studio: per-application audio (stream a game and your microphone while music and voice chat stay private), streaming to several platforms at once from one encode, and staying light on older PCs. This version captures a whole display, a single window or a camera or capture card into a live preview, where you arrange them in scenes, and captures sound: microphones and other inputs, desktop audio (everything you hear), and on Windows 11 one program's sound on its own. Each sound source has a meter, a volume fader and mute, and its own routing: to the stream, to any of six recording tracks, and to your headphones, so a game can go to viewers while music and voice chat stay in your ears only. Capture warns when something kept off the stream would still reach it through desktop audio, or when monitoring would feed back or echo. This version adds recording: H.264 video from your graphics card's encoder (or Windows' software one) and up to six AAC audio tracks, in MKV files that stay playable if the PC crashes. Recording has been checked here only as far as the file format (against ffmpeg); it hasn't yet been tried with a real encoder, so please report how it goes. This version adds streaming to Twitch, YouTube, Kick or any RTMP or RTMPS server, with the stream key kept in Windows Credential Manager and automatic reconnection if the connection drops. Streaming has been checked here against a local test server, not yet with a real encoder or a real platform, so please report how it goes.

Features

  • Capture a whole display (Desktop Duplication, with Windows Graphics Capture and a compatibility mode as fallbacks), with or without the mouse pointer
  • Capture one window, even when it's covered (Windows Graphics Capture); it's found again when the program restarts
  • Webcams and capture cards that Windows sees as cameras
  • Sound: microphones and other inputs, desktop audio (everything an output device plays), and on Windows 11 one program's sound on its own (a game without your music or voice chat), each found again when the device or program comes back
  • An audio mixer with level meters, a volume fader and mute for each sound source, and meters for the stream and your headphones
  • Routing: send each sound source to the stream, recording tracks 1 to 6 and the monitor (your headphones) independently; anything not sent to the stream is absent from it
  • Warnings when a program kept off the stream would still be heard through desktop audio (with a one-click fix: leave it out of desktop audio, on Windows 11), when something would feed back, or when a microphone could pick up the monitor
  • Save a 10-second test clip of the stream mix, any track or the monitor, to hear exactly what it carries
  • Save a 10-second test clip of any sound source's own sound, to hear exactly what it captures
  • A live preview: drag to move, drag the handles to resize (Shift for any shape), Alt+drag to crop, with snapping to the canvas edges and centre (hold Ctrl to stop it)
  • Fit, stretch, centre, rotate, mirror, reset; hide, lock and reorder sources; the arrow keys nudge
  • Scenes: add, rename, duplicate, reorder and delete, switch with Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9; a source can be shared by several scenes and is captured once
  • The workspace: resize, rearrange, hide and save the panels as layouts
  • Light on the computer: when the preview is hidden and nothing is recording or streaming, nothing is composed
  • Settings: theme, low-resource mode, preview and output frame rates, canvas size (1080p, 720p, 1440p or vertical 9:16); the encoders your PC offers
  • Recording to MKV: H.264 video through NVIDIA, AMD or Intel hardware encoders (or Windows' software encoder) and up to six AAC audio tracks named after what's on them; playable even if the PC crashes (new, not yet tried on real hardware)
  • A sync test source (a white flash and a beep at each second) for checking picture and sound line up in a recording
  • Streaming to Twitch, YouTube, Kick or a custom RTMP/RTMPS server, with the stream key kept in Windows Credential Manager, the server's certificate checked, and automatic reconnection (new, not yet tried with a real platform)
  • Not yet: streaming to several platforms at once, and saving recordings as MP4 (each arrives in a later version)

What's new

Version 0.6.0 · 2026-11-14

  • Streaming: Start streaming in Controls. Choose Twitch, YouTube, Kick or a custom server in Settings › Streaming, and enter your stream key.
  • The stream key is kept in Windows Credential Manager on this PC. It's never shown, and never written to settings or logs. A key pasted on the end of a server address is moved there too.
  • Secure streaming (RTMPS) uses Windows' own TLS, and Capture checks the server's certificate itself: a wrong name or an untrusted authority is refused, with the reason.
  • If the connection drops, Capture keeps going and reconnects by itself (after 2 seconds, then longer, up to a minute). A stream key the platform refuses isn't retried. If the network can't keep up, old frames are dropped rather than the stream falling behind, and counted in the status bar.
  • The status bar shows the stream's state, time, bitrate and dropped frames. Recording and streaming can run together.
  • Please note: streaming has been tested here against a local test server, over plain and secure connections. This computer has no H.264 or AAC encoders, so it hasn't yet run with a real encoder or to a real platform. If it doesn't work, the message and the log (Settings › Open the logs folder) say why.
  • Not yet: streaming to several platforms at once (next) and saving a recording as MP4.

Version 0.5.0 · 2026-11-14

  • Recording: Start recording in Controls. The canvas is encoded as H.264 by your graphics card's encoder where it has one (NVIDIA, AMD or Intel), or by Windows' software encoder, with the recording tracks chosen in Settings › Recording as AAC audio tracks, each named after the sources on it.
  • Recordings are MKV files in Videos\NorthRay Capture (or the folder you choose). They're written so that everything up to a crash or power cut still plays. Recording stops cleanly if the disk is nearly full.
  • Settings › Recording: the folder, the video encoder (automatic or a chosen one), video quality (6 to 40 Mbit/s), which audio tracks go in the file, and audio quality.
  • While recording: the time in Controls, and the time, the file's size and any lost frames in the status bar.
  • A sync test source (+ in Sources › Sync test): a white flash and a beep at the start of each second, for checking that picture and sound line up in a recording.
  • Please note: recording has only been tested here as far as the file format. This computer has no H.264 or AAC encoders, so recording hasn't yet run with a real encoder. If it doesn't work on your PC, the message and the log (Settings › Open the logs folder) say why.
  • Not yet: streaming (next) and saving a recording as MP4.

Version 0.4.0 · 2026-11-14

  • Audio routing: each sound source can be sent to the stream (what viewers hear), recording tracks 1 to 6, and the monitor (what you hear) independently, with the buttons under its fader or “Send to” in its right-click menu. A source that isn't sent to the stream is absent from it, not just turned down.
  • New sources start on the stream and track 1 (everything), plus a track of their own kind: microphones on 2, desktop audio on 3, programs on 4, ready for editing later.
  • Monitoring: the monitor mix plays on the device chosen in Settings › Audio (Windows' default to begin with). The mixer shows meters for the stream and the monitor.
  • Warnings in the mixer when a program kept off the stream would still reach viewers through desktop audio, when the same sound reaches a mix twice, when monitoring desktop audio would feed back into itself, and when a microphone could pick up monitoring played through speakers. A leak can be fixed in one click (on Windows 11): desktop audio leaves that program out.
  • Desktop audio can leave one program out (right-click › Leave a program out, Windows 11). Windows allows one program per desktop audio source.
  • Settings › Audio lists where each sound source goes, and any problems.
  • Save a 10-second test clip of the stream mix, any track or the monitor (right-click a sound source › Save a test clip of a mix).
  • The mixer scrolls with the mouse wheel when its sources don't all fit.

Version 0.3.0 · 2026-11-14

  • Sound capture: add a microphone or other input, desktop audio (everything an output device plays) or, on Windows 11, one program's sound on its own from + in the Audio mixer (or in Sources). A program is found by name, so its sound is captured again whenever it restarts; sources set to Windows' default device follow it when it changes.
  • The Audio mixer: a level meter for each sound source and for the mix (green, yellow and red, with a line at the recent peak), a volume fader showing decibels, and a mute button. Sound sources are heard in every scene.
  • Right-click a sound source to rename it, change its device or program, mute it, remove it, or save a 10-second test clip of just its own sound (before its volume and mute) to Videos\NorthRay Capture, so you can hear exactly what it captures.
  • Click a sound source's name to see its whole status, such as why a device can't be used.
  • Not yet: separate stream, recording and headphone mixes (next), recording and streaming.
