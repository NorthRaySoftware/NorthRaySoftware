About

NorthRay Capture is a livestreaming and recording app for Windows, being built in stages towards a real alternative to OBS Studio: per-application audio (stream a game and your microphone while music and voice chat stay private), streaming to several platforms at once from one encode, and staying light on older PCs. This version captures a whole display, a single window or a camera or capture card into a live preview, where you arrange them in scenes, and captures sound: microphones and other inputs, desktop audio (everything you hear), and on Windows 11 one program's sound on its own. Each sound source has a meter, a volume fader and mute, and its own routing: to the stream, to any of six recording tracks, and to your headphones, so a game can go to viewers while music and voice chat stay in your ears only. Capture warns when something kept off the stream would still reach it through desktop audio, or when monitoring would feed back or echo. This version adds recording: H.264 video from your graphics card's encoder (or Windows' software one) and up to six AAC audio tracks, in MKV files that stay playable if the PC crashes. Recording has been checked here only as far as the file format (against ffmpeg); it hasn't yet been tried with a real encoder, so please report how it goes. It streams to Twitch, YouTube, Kick or any RTMP or RTMPS server, and to several of them at once from one encode, with the stream key kept in Windows Credential Manager and automatic reconnection if the connection drops. Streaming has been checked here against a local test server, not yet with a real encoder or a real platform, so please report how it goes.

Features

  • Capture a whole display (Desktop Duplication, with Windows Graphics Capture and a compatibility mode as fallbacks), with or without the mouse pointer
  • Capture one window, even when it's covered (Windows Graphics Capture); it's found again when the program restarts
  • Webcams and capture cards that Windows sees as cameras
  • Sound: microphones and other inputs, desktop audio (everything an output device plays), and on Windows 11 one program's sound on its own (a game without your music or voice chat), each found again when the device or program comes back
  • An audio mixer with level meters, a volume fader and mute for each sound source, and meters for the stream and your headphones
  • Audio filters on any sound source: gain, noise gate, noise suppression, expander, compressor, limiter, equaliser, balance and delay, with presets for a microphone or a game
  • Optional audio-effect plugins (a documented C interface with an example): off until you switch each one on after seeing its SHA-256 and signature, and switched off again if the file changes
  • Each stream destination can carry its own sound (the stream mix or any recording track), and a second output plays any mix on another device, for a two-PC setup
  • A camera border maker: rounded corners or a circle, a plain or gradient ring and a glow or shadow on any source, edited live, and saved as a PNG overlay if you want
  • An overlay designer: text, shapes and pictures with gradients, outlines, shadows and entrance animations, snapping guides and layers, ready-made Starting Soon, Be Right Back, Ending, lower third, facecam frame and panel templates in the NorthRay look, and PNG export
  • Timers in designs (countdowns to a time or for a length, stopwatch, time live, clock) that can switch scenes at zero, QR codes from your links, and built-in icons
  • A brand kit: your colours, heading and body fonts (including font files that aren't installed) and logo; designs and templates that use it change when it does
  • Live Twitch chat in overlays and in a Chat panel: read-only and without signing in; deleted messages, timeouts and bans disappear too
  • Now playing in overlays: the song and artist of the music you play, from its tags or file name
  • Music from a folder, shuffled, with auto-duck: it dips smoothly while your microphone has sound
  • An MP4 copy of each recording (no re-encoding; the MKV is kept), with markers as chapters
  • Profiles and scene collections, switched from the top of the window; recordings named after the profile
  • A setup wizard with a Twitch bandwidth test, suggesting settings for your PC and connection
  • A soundboard with 12 starter sounds made by NorthRay (free to use) and your own files
  • Voice effects: reverb, pitch shift and a radio voice, as filters on any sound
  • Green screen on any item, with a colour picker that reads your screen from the preview
  • The aurora sweep transition, in your brand colours
  • Save a design as a looping animated GIF of its entrance
  • An optional pre-live checklist: what Capture checked (keys, mic, scene, encoder) and your own list
  • Automation: switch scenes when a program opens or closes
  • Global hotkeys for scenes, streaming, recording, markers, mute and the soundboard
  • The aurora sky: a live animated background drawn on the graphics card, in your brand colours
  • Facecam frames that move and resize with the camera (any item can follow another)
  • Stingers, intros and outros kept in the brand kit
  • Scene templates: gaming, just chatting, art, reading and writing, radio show
  • Aurora pulse and Aurora wave: animated aurora backgrounds that move with your music
  • A book card that shows the book you're writing in Writing Studio, with its cover
  • A Writing progress overlay (the chapter and its words, today's words against your goal, the book's words, your streak), kept in step with Writing Studio automatically
  • The aurora pulse: an aurora background that moves with your music
  • The NorthRay library: one media library for the suite, with folders, tags and search
  • Template packs: share a folder of your library as one file
  • Routing: send each sound source to the stream, recording tracks 1 to 6 and the monitor (your headphones) independently; anything not sent to the stream is absent from it
  • Warnings when a program kept off the stream would still be heard through desktop audio (with a one-click fix: leave it out of desktop audio, on Windows 11), when something would feed back, or when a microphone could pick up the monitor
  • Save a 10-second test clip of the stream mix, any track or the monitor, to hear exactly what it carries
  • Save a 10-second test clip of any sound source's own sound, to hear exactly what it captures
  • A live preview: drag to move, drag the handles to resize (Shift for any shape), Alt+drag to crop, with snapping to the canvas edges and centre (hold Ctrl to stop it)
  • Fit, stretch, centre, rotate, mirror, reset; hide, lock and reorder sources; the arrow keys nudge
  • Scenes: add, rename, duplicate, reorder and delete, switch with Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9; a source can be shared by several scenes and is captured once; put scenes in folders; export a scene to a file and import it, here or on another PC
  • Transitions between scenes: cut, fade, slide, swipe, luma wipe (six patterns), a stinger video, or your own shader, with their duration and easing
  • Studio Mode: prepare the next scene in the Preview while the Program stays live, then put it live with a transition (Ctrl+Enter)
  • Scenes inside scenes: show one scene inside another, placed like any source (never a scene inside itself)
  • Images (animated GIFs play; the picture updates when the file changes), text with font, colour, outline and wrapping, colours, slideshows from a folder, and any part of the screen
  • Video and sound files, one or a playlist, with their sound in the mixer; group sources to move and hide them as one
  • Exact controls for every source: type its position and size or rotation, set its opacity, duplicate it
  • The workspace: resize, rearrange, hide and save the panels as layouts
  • Light on the computer: when the preview is hidden and nothing is recording or streaming, nothing is composed
  • Settings: theme, low-resource mode, preview and output frame rates, canvas size (1080p, 720p, 1440p or vertical 9:16); the encoders your PC offers
  • Recording to MKV: H.264 video through NVIDIA, AMD or Intel hardware encoders (or Windows' software encoder) and up to six AAC audio tracks named after what's on them; playable even if the PC crashes (new, not yet tried on real hardware)
  • A sync test source (a white flash and a beep at each second) for checking picture and sound line up in a recording
  • Streaming to Twitch, YouTube, Kick or a custom RTMP/RTMPS server, with the stream key kept in Windows Credential Manager, the server's certificate checked, and automatic reconnection (new, not yet tried with a real platform)
  • Multistreaming: up to six destinations at once without a restreaming service; destinations at the same bitrate share one encode; each has its own buffer, reconnection and status, and starts or stops on its own (new, not yet tried with a real platform)
  • Not yet: web pages as sources, vertical canvases per destination (each in a later version)

What's new

Version 0.15.0 · 2026-11-20

  • A gradient bar under every gradient fill in the designer, with a handle for each colour: click the bar to add a colour there, drag a handle to move it (it snaps to tenths; hold Shift for anywhere), pull it off the bar to remove it, or click it to change its colour. A Direction slider turns the gradient.
  • Glow: light round text, shapes and pictures, in any colour (your brand's main colour to start), with its size and strength. It goes round outlines too.
  • Text outlines are now grown from the letters themselves: even all round, with rounded corners, at any thickness. Shadows and glows go round the outline.
  • Gradient text runs across the words themselves rather than the whole text box, so a short title gets every colour of its gradient.
  • The designer's properties panel scrolls with the mouse wheel when it's taller than the window.

Version 0.14.0 · 2026-11-20

  • Select several elements in the designer: shift-click to add or take one away, drag a box round them from an empty part of the canvas, Ctrl+A for everything and Esc for nothing. Move them together, nudge them with the arrow keys, duplicate or delete them at once.
  • Line up and space evenly: line a selection up by its left, centre, right, top, middle or bottom, space three or more evenly across or down, or centre it on the page.
  • Groups (Ctrl+G, Ctrl+Shift+G to ungroup): grouped elements select and move as one, show a coloured bar in Layers, and Alt-click picks one inside to change on its own.
  • Rulers and guides: click a ruler to place a guide, drag a guide to move it, and drag it back onto the ruler (or off the page) to remove it; Page › Remove the guides clears them all. Page › Grid gives a snapping grid of 10, 20, 40, 50 or 100 pixels, used when no other element or guide is near (hold Shift not to snap).

Version 0.13.0 · 2026-11-20

  • Writing Studio, live on stream: new designs for a sprint timer (shows only during a writing sprint), the viewpoint character's card, a cast list of who's in the chapter, and a chapter card with its status, place, story day and target.
  • Celebrations: the Celebration design pops up when you reach your daily word goal or finish a sprint.
  • Settings › Writing Studio: spoiler-safe (leaves the story's details out of every design), your book's world colour on brand-linked designs, a recording marker for each chapter, and scenes that follow the part of Writing Studio you're in.
  • {streamwords}: the words you've written this stream. The stream report lists the words, chapters and sprints written during it.
  • Fixed: timers set to Until going live, Time left in the stream or Next stream turned back into plain countdowns when Capture was reopened.

Version 0.12.0 · 2026-11-19

  • Highlight the pointer (right-click a display, window or screen-region source): a soft halo round the pointer and a ring from each click, right clicks in a second colour. Size, halo strength and colours to choose.
  • Time-lapse recording (Settings › Recording): one frame every 1, 2, 5, 10 or 30 seconds, played back at your frame rate, with no sound. The record button says Start time-lapse while it's on.
  • A Notes panel (Layout › Notes): write your talking points or script; it's saved as you type. Read shows them large and scrolls them like a teleprompter, at the speed and size you choose. Only you see it.
  • What each destination gets: a page with each stream destination's picture, bitrate, shared encode, sound mix with its live meter, and how it's doing while live. From Controls or Settings › Streaming.
  • Do not disturb: Windows only lets Microsoft-approved apps switch it on, so Capture reminds you when you go live and opens Windows' notification settings, where it can turn on by itself for games or full-screen apps.
